## 1. The problem this stage solves

Before the optimiser places a single charger, the system must decide **which candidate sites
may receive one at all**, and it must say why for every site. A planner who sees a site left off
a plan needs to know whether it was ruled out by government policy, by arithmetic, by our own
assumption, or simply because a fact was missing.

The proposal commits to four outcomes: **accept**, **reject**, **defer** and **conditional**.
Two of them must never be confused:

- **Reject** is a judgement: the evidence shows the site should get no more points.
- **Defer** is a data gap: a fact needed to judge the site is missing.

Collapsing them would punish sites for gaps in the data rather than for anything about the site.
That distinction drives most of the design below.

## 2. The rule register

The rules are data, not code. `register.toml` holds one entry per rule: its condition in plain
language, its **source class**, a **source reference**, the facts it needs, an explanation
template, and the outcome it gives when it fails (`on_fail`). `load_register` validates the file
and refuses it with every problem listed at once, so a bad edit cannot reach the engine. It
rejects:
- unknown facts
- a template that uses a fact the rule doesn't declare
- a `source_ref` whose prefix doesn't match its class (`ds-` dataset, `doc-` document, `asm-` assumption)
- duplicate ids

| Rule | Condition | Source class | Source | Fails → |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | Car capacity is published for the site | Government dataset | data.gov.sg, Car Park Availability (HDB) | reject |
| R02 | A basement car park needs its fire-safety provisions for EV charging confirmed first | Government policy | MHA written parliamentary reply, 8 Sep 2026 | conditional |
| R03 | Existing points plus one new point stay within 15% of the car lots (`MAX_SHARE_OF_LOTS`) | Arithmetic limit | LTA factsheet, 4 Mar 2021 | reject |
| R04 | Sites whose chargers are idle (used under 5% of the time, `IDLE_BELOW`) get no more points | Our declared assumption | `asm-idle-threshold` | reject |
| R05 | The site lies inside a Master Plan 2025 planning area | Government dataset | URA Master Plan 2025 Planning Area Boundary | reject |
| R06 | Electrical capacity must be confirmed before points are added where no charger runs yet | Government policy | MOT written parliamentary reply, 14 Oct 2025 | conditional |

**Where each rule comes from.** Every source was read and checked before it was cited.

- **R01** reads `total_lots` from the HDB availability feed collected for a week (§8.1a). The HDB
  registry carries no lot counts, so the feed is their only source.
- **R02.** The MHA reply on fire safety standards for EVs in enclosed and multi-storey car parks
  states that "sprinklers are required for enclosed basement carparks", and that SCDF mandates
  "at least one emergency main isolation shut-off switch … within 15 metres of an electric
  vehicle (EV) charging station". No dataset says whether a given basement has these, so a
  basement car park is conditional on confirming them.
- **R03.** LTA's factsheet says new HDB car parks will "cater sufficient electrical capacity to
  support EV slow charging for 15 per cent of their car parking lots". We use that share as the
  ceiling on points per site.
- **R04** is our own judgement, declared in `optev.assumptions` with its value and reason: adding
  points where existing chargers sit idle does not relieve anyone.
- **R05** relies on conform assigning every site a planning area by point-in-polygon on the
  MP2025 boundaries.
- **R06.** The MOT reply commits to scaling up chargers "in carparks with high charging demand,
  electrical capacity permitting". No public dataset reports spare electrical capacity. We
  therefore treat it as demonstrated only where chargers already run, and as to be confirmed
  everywhere else.

All four source classes appear. An explanation (§8.1d) can therefore tell a planner whether a site
failed government policy, a published dataset, an arithmetic limit or a project assumption. A
similarity search over documents cannot make that distinction.

## 3. How a verdict is decided

**Facts.** For each site the engine gathers the facts the design allows:
- `kind`, `planning_area`, `total_lots`, `existing_connectors`
- `car_park_type`
- `supply_state` and `utilisation` (from the indicators)
- `land_use`, `amenities_nearby` and `nearest_point_m`

A missing value is **absent**, never 0. Utilisation at a site with no chargers is undefined, not
zero (project invariant 2).

**Forward chaining.** Derived facts are added while their inputs are present. For example,
`room = floor(0.15 × total_lots) − existing_connectors`, the points a site can still take. The
checks then read the extended facts.

**Every rule is evaluated**, never only up to the first failure, so a planner sees the whole
picture. Each rule gives one result:

- **passed**, or **failed**, with a detail such as `room 0`;
- **unevaluated**, when a fact it needs is missing (`total_lots missing`).

Two refinements:
- **A check can also return unevaluated** when it needs a fact only for some kinds of site. R02
  reads `car_park_type` only for car parks. A hawker centre passes ("not a car park"), but a car
  park of unknown type defers and is never assumed safe.
- **A register rule with no check is an error**, never a silent defer.

**Outcome policy**, applied in this order (§4.4):

1. any failed rule whose `on_fail` is reject → **reject**;
2. otherwise, any unevaluated rule → **defer**;
3. otherwise, any failed conditional rule → **conditional**;
4. otherwise → **accept**.

A missing fact alone therefore never rejects. A data gap outranks a conditional failure, because
"confirm the electrical capacity" is not actionable while the site's lot count is unknown.

**Reasons survive.** Each decision stores every rule's result and detail (`rule_result`). It
round-trips through the frozen `Decision` contract. It is also what the knowledge graph links to
each rule's source (§8.1d).

## 4. Screening and what the allocator receives

`screen` runs the engine over every candidate site, each with its latest indicators window. It
returns the decisions and the **funnel**, the count per outcome. `write_decisions` stores them
per (site, scenario), replacing earlier results, so a rerun gives the same tables. The command
`python -m optev.reasoning screen [--dry-run]` screens the Postgres tables directly. The
allocator (§8.1c) reads only the **eligible set: accept and conditional**.

**On the sample (30 sites):** 8 accept · 17 conditional · 3 defer · 2 reject.

| Outcome | Sites | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reject | 2 | One car park has no room left within 15% of its lots (R03). One site's chargers are idle (R04). |
| Defer | 3 | Two amenity sites and a petrol station have no lot count (R01, R03 unevaluated). |
| Conditional | 17 | 15 have no charger yet, so electrical capacity must be confirmed (R06). One basement already has chargers (R02). One basement has neither (R02 and R06). |
| Accept | 8 | Chargers already run, there is room, they are in use, and the site is not a basement. |

## 5. The Rules page

The Rules page (`app/pages/2_Rules.py`, WBS 6.4a) explains every decision from the screen alone:
- the register, with each rule's source type, title and link
- the funnel
- every site's decision, filterable by outcome and area
- for one site, each rule's result and detail

Missing data reads as "unevaluated", never as failed. For each failed rule the page also shows
**what waiving that rule alone would change**, computed with the engine's own outcome function.
For example, waiving R03 at a full car park turns its reject into accept. That is the "would one
exception change the result?" question the WBS asked for.

## 6. What we tried that did not work

- **Choosing rules by data availability.** The first register kept only rules whose facts the
  collected week provided. It dropped R05 (planning areas weren't loaded yet) and R02 (no car
  park type). Review rejected this, rightly: a rule belongs in the register because a source
  supports it. Missing data should only make the rule defer. Both rules came back once the
  principle was written into the design (v1.15).
- **A citation that did not say what we needed.** The first R02 cited LTA's page on regulating
  EV charger safety, which is in the proposal's references. On reading it, the page says nothing
  about basements. SCDF's Fire Code clause on EV charging covers isolation switches for every car
  park, not basements in particular. The rule came back only when the MHA reply of 8 Sep 2026 gave
  a basement-specific requirement. Even then, we wrote the condition as "confirm the provisions",
  not "basements need a review", because no source says the latter.
- **One rule can dominate the funnel.** R06 accounts for 15 of the 17 conditional sample sites.
  Reading "electrical capacity permitting" as "shown only where chargers already run" is
  defensible but strict: no site without chargers can be accepted. It does not change what the
  allocator may choose, since conditional sites remain eligible. It does change what "accept"
  means, so we flag it for the planner rather than hide it.

## 7. Checking the rules against people

**Method (§6, Stage 4).** We drew 30 real candidate sites, stratified by the system's outcome
(8 accept, 8 conditional, 7 defer, 7 reject). The rating sheet
(`ProjectReport/results/rules-check-sheet.csv`) shows only each site's facts: kind, planning
area, car lots, existing chargers, car park type, supply state, utilisation, land use, nearby
amenities and distance to the nearest charger. It never shows the system's verdict. Each rater
gave accept, conditional, defer or reject, reading only the rule register and the rater guide
(`ProjectReport/results/rules-check-guide.md`). `optev/evaluation/rules_check.py` writes the
sheet (`sheet`) and scores it (`score`), and is tested on hand-checked κ values.

**Raters.** Neither wrote the rules. **Rater A** was an AI assistant (Claude) applying the guide
to the sheet; we disclose this because it is not the human rater the design asks for. **Rater
B** was Sushant, rating a blank copy without seeing rater A or the app.

**Results** (`ProjectReport/results/rules-check.md`):

| Measure | Value | Target (§6) |
|---|---|---|
| Cohen's κ between raters | 1.00 | ≥ 0.7 |
| Rater A agrees with the system | 100% (κ 1.00) | 100% |
| Rater B agrees with the system | 100% (κ 1.00) | 100% |
| Where the raters agree, the system agrees | 100% | 100% |
| Verdicts with a reason code | 100% | 100% |

Every target is met. Every stored decision carries a result and detail for every rule, so the
reason-code target is met by construction.

**Disagreements.** None: both raters matched each other and the system on all 30 sites.

**Caveat.** Perfect agreement is partly a property of the rules: they read published fields
and declared thresholds, so a careful rater following the register reaches the same verdict.
The check shows the rules are applied as written and are unambiguous to a reader. It does not
show the rules are the right ones; that rests on the sources in §3. One rater being an AI
assistant also weakens the check against the design's intent of two people.

## 8. Limitations

- **Electrical capacity and fire safety are unknowable from public data.** R02 and R06
  therefore make sites conditional rather than accepting or rejecting them. A real deployment
  would replace them with a feed from the grid operator and building records.
- **R05 cannot reject in practice.** A site outside every planning-area polygon has no area, so
  R05 defers it as a data gap. That is the right call for points on reclaimed land or a boundary.
- **The idle threshold (R04) is ours.** It is declared with its value and reason, and a planner
  may weigh it differently.
- **Rules are evaluated per site, not per scenario.** Growth scenarios change demand, not
  eligibility, so one baseline decision serves every scenario unless a scenario stores its own.
